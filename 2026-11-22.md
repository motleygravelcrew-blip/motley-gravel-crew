## 02 --- ROAD

### 24 teams launch Collective Cycling Group --- a new attempt to reshape pro cycling's economics

**18 November:** 24 professional teams across the men's and women's
WorldTour and ProTeam categories signed a shareholder agreement to
establish the **Collective Cycling Group (CCG)**.

The team-owned company says it wants to develop and market selected
commercial rights and activities, including teams' media and data
assets, merchandising, hospitality and fan experiences. Its stated goal
is to create more revenue opportunities and a more sustainable business
model for professional road cycling.

The full list of the 24 founding teams has not been published in the
launch statement. Some teams have publicly confirmed their involvement,
but reported membership lists and breakdowns vary between media reports.

**Why it matters:** professional teams carry major operating costs but
have limited control over some of the sport's biggest commercial assets.
A coordinated team-owned group could give them more collective
bargaining power. It is not yet a new race series, a replacement for the
UCI or a settled reform package. The next test is how CCG works with
race organisers and the governing body.

The UCI is pursuing its own reform process, with a blueprint expected at
the WorldTour seminar in Benidorm on **9 December**.

Sources: [NSN Cycling Team --- official CCG announcement, 18
November](https://nsncyclingteam.com/a-shared-commitment-to-the-future-of-professional-cycling/)

### TotalEnergies exits Netcompany-Ineos partnership, but stays in women's cycling

**19 November:** TotalEnergies confirmed it will end its sponsorship of
Netcompany-Ineos after 18 months. The French energy company will instead
become co-title sponsor of the women's FDJ United-Suez team under a
five-year agreement from 2027, with the team expected to race as
**TotalEnergies-FDJ**.

The Netcompany-Ineos team says it valued the partnership across two
seasons. Netcompany remains the team's title sponsor.

**MGC take:** this is a sponsor transfer rather than a complete
withdrawal from cycling. It also underlines how quickly team budgets and
sponsorship structures can change between seasons.

Source: [Cycling Weekly --- TotalEnergies ends Netcompany-Ineos
sponsorship](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/racing/totalenergies-ends-netcompany-ineos-sponsorship-after-18-months)

### 2027 Santos Tour Down Under: men and women share the same stages

The 2027 Santos Tour Down Under has confirmed a new format: the men's
and women's races will use the **same course, on the same day and over
the same distance** on their overlapping race days.

The men race from **19--24 January**. The women's three-stage race runs
from **22--24 January**, sharing the route on those final three days.

**Why it matters:** the format gives both fields the same terrain and
stage challenge, while creating a more integrated race-day experience
for spectators and broadcasters. It is a notable step in the
presentation of elite men's and women's racing.

Sources: [Santos Tour Down Under --- 2027 race
format](https://tourdownunder.com.au/race) · [UCI --- 2027 event
listing](https://www.uci.org/competition-details/2027/ROA/79669)

## 04 --- TECHNOLOGY

### Amflow takes its Avinox technology from the trails into the city

**17 November:** Amflow announced the UE Carbon Series, its first
dedicated commuter e-bike range, with the UE Carbon and UE Carbon Pro.

The manufacturer lists a full-carbon frame, a claimed complete-bike
weight of **20.9kg**, a removable **600Wh** battery and its Avinox M2
drive system. The Pro uses a three-speed electronic internal hub and
Gates belt drive; the standard Carbon uses a 10-speed electronic
derailleur. Both models add connected features including Apple Find My
integration.

**Why it matters:** Amflow is taking technology developed in the
performance e-MTB space into urban transport. The
belt-drive/internal-hub Pro model is particularly interesting as a
low-maintenance commuter concept.

**Australian caveat:** the manufacturer's launch material does not
establish the exact Australian-market motor configuration or road-use
compliance. The headline peak-power and torque figures should not be
treated as proof that a particular model is legal as a standard pedelec
on Australian public roads. Buyers should confirm local specification
and compliance before purchase.

Source: [Amflow --- official UE Series launch, 17
November](https://www.amflowbikes.com/news/release-ue-carbon)
