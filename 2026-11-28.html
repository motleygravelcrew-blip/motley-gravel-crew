Saturday, 28 November 2026 · Perth, Western Australia

Saturday Special Edition · Road · Gravel · Cyclocross · XCO/XCM · Australian Cycling

THE MGC READ
This Saturday special has a clear message for Australian gravel riders: the 2027 calendar is taking shape, and Western Australia again has a major part to play. SEVEN returns to Nannup as a UCI Gravel World Series qualifier, the new Slow SEVEN ride adds a different way to experience the course, Mt Marshall returns to the calendar, and the Gravel National Championships head to Nabawa and the Chapman Valley. Internationally, the cyclocross season has opened in the snow at Ostrava, while Wout van Aert's return is now on the horizon.

01 — GRAVEL: 2027 SEASON TAKES SHAPE

AusCycling releases its 2027 gravel calendar — and WA is firmly on it

27 November: AusCycling has released its 2027 gravel calendar, setting out a season that spans local participation events, state and national championships, and UCI Gravel World Series qualification. For Western Australian riders, the calendar includes a strong run of events from the Blackwood Valley through the Wheatbelt and into the Mid West.

The headline event for many will be SEVEN Gravel Race on 15 May at Nannup. SEVEN returns as a UCI Gravel World Series round, giving riders the opportunity to qualify for the 2027 UCI Gravel World Championships in Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc, France. It is one of two Australian qualifying opportunities listed for 2027, alongside The Devils Cardigan in Tasmania on 19 June. The Devils Cardigan will also host the Oceania Gravel Championships for the first time.

There is also a new addition for riders who want a different experience of the SEVEN course. Slow SEVEN is scheduled for 9 October, taking riders through the seven sectors in reverse, at a more relaxed pace, with regroup stops at the major summits. It is designed around exploration and discovery rather than a conventional race format.

Western Australian dates to put in the diary:

  • 3 April — Rotary Tour of the Blackwood, Bridgetown
  • 10–11 April — Tiger Tracks Gravel, Jalbarragup
  • 15 May — SEVEN Gravel Race, Nannup (UCI Gravel World Series)
  • 10 July — Mt Marshall Bendigo Bank Gravel Grind, Bencubbin
  • 7 August — AusCycling Gravel National Championships, Nabawa and the Chapman Valley
  • 18 September — Bike it to Ballidu Gravel, Ballidu
  • 9 October — Slow SEVEN, Nannup
  • 23 October — Wild Gravel, Gnowangerup

Why it matters: this is more than a list of race dates. It gives WA riders a connected season of opportunities — from local gravel weekends and established events to world-championship qualification and a national title event on home roads. The August national championships will bring the green-and-gold jersey contest to the Mid West for the first time.

Sources: AusCycling — Choose your own adventure with the 2027 gravel calendar, 27 November · AusCycling — 2027 gravel event calendar · AusCycling — Slow SEVEN event details

02 — CYCLOCROSS: THE WORLD CUP IS UNDER WAY

Schreiber and Nys win a snowy World Cup opener in Ostrava

The 2026–27 UCI Cyclo-cross World Cup opened in Ostrava, Czechia, on Friday 27 November, with snow and difficult conditions providing an immediate test for the field.

In the elite women's race, Marie Schreiber took the victory ahead of Aniek van Alphen and Manon Bakker. Lucinda Brand, the reigning world champion, finished 15th after a difficult race that included an apparent pedal issue and a late crash.

The elite men's race went to Thibau Nys, who edged Tibor Del Grosso in a close finish. Michael Vanthourenhout completed the podium in third, with Cameron Mason fourth.

The series moves straight on to Tábor on Sunday 29 November. The Czech double-header gives riders little time to reset after the opening round and should provide a useful early picture of form as the winter campaign develops.

Why it matters: the opening round has already delivered two different kinds of racing — a solo women's win and a tight men's finish — and the snowy conditions showed how quickly cyclocross can become a test of bike handling as much as raw speed.

Sources: Cyclingnews — Marie Schreiber wins the women's opener · Cyclingnews — Thibau Nys wins the men's opener · UCI — 2026–27 World Cup calendar

03 — ROAD & CYCLOCROSS: VAN AERT SETS A RETURN DATE

Wout van Aert expected back in cyclocross at Antwerp on 19 December

28 November: Wout van Aert is set to return to cyclocross competition at the World Cup round in Antwerp on 19 December, according to reporting by Cyclingnews. The Belgian's winter programme is expected to be broader than last season, when injury cut his campaign short.

There is growing expectation that the programme could include a tilt at the UCI Cyclo-cross World Championships in Ostend on 31 January 2027. That target has been discussed in the reporting, but the final race programme should be treated as provisional until confirmed by the rider and team.

Why it matters: Van Aert's return adds another major name to a cyclocross season that has already begun with strong racing. His presence would lift the depth and interest of the Christmas block and could set up another major championship contest in January.

Source: Cyclingnews — Wout van Aert to make cyclocross return in Antwerp, 28 November

04 — THE WEEKEND WATCH

Racing continues across Australia's cycling calendar

The final weekend of November has a mix of grassroots and state-level racing on the AusCycling calendar. The Blackline Track League Round 3 is scheduled in Adelaide across 28–29 November, while the 2026 AusCycling MTB Cross-Country State Championships are being held in Queensland over the same weekend, hosted by Ipswich Offroad Cyclists.

For riders looking beyond this weekend, the release of the 2027 gravel calendar is the key planning update. WA riders now have dates for a broad spread of events, with more detailed event information and entry arrangements to follow through the organisers.

Sources: AusCycling — current events and news · AusCycling — gravel calendar
