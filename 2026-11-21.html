02 — ROAD

NSN turns to Cube for its 2027 WorldTour campaign

19 November: NSN Cycling Team confirmed Cube as its bike partner for 2027, with Newmen wheels, SRAM groupsets and Selle Italia saddles. The team is set to use Cube’s Litening C:68X Aero and Air road bikes, plus the Aerium C:68X for time trials.

It is the team’s third bike supplier in three seasons, following Factor and Scott. With Primož Roglič, Richard Carapaz and Kasper Asgreen among the 2027 names, the partnership arrives alongside a stated greater focus on general classification.

MGC take: equipment partnerships are moving quickly as teams rebuild for 2027. The more interesting test will be whether the new package brings stability and performance across a full season, rather than just a fresh launch-day look.

Source: Cyclingnews — NSN switches to Cube for 2027

Vuelta Ciclista a Canarias delayed until 2028

17 November: organisers postponed the planned four-day Canary Islands stage race, which had been scheduled for 14–17 January 2027. They cited insufficient time to guarantee the organisational, logistical and safety requirements for an event of that scale. The inaugural edition is now intended for January 2028.

This is a postponement, not a confirmed permanent cancellation. It removes one proposed early-season European race from the 2027 calendar.

Source: Cyclingnews — Vuelta Ciclista a Canarias postponed

2027 WorldTour: Australia gets the opening move

The UCI’s published 2027 men’s WorldTour calendar lists 36 events across 13 countries and 169 race days. The season opens with the Santos Tour Down Under, 19–24 January, followed by the Men’s Cadel Evans Great Ocean Road Race on 31 January.

The UCI has also approved two safety measures for 2027: extending the yellow-card system to Elite men’s and women’s Class 1 events, and introducing more consistent hazard pictograms for features such as narrowings, sharp turns, speed bumps and level crossings.

Source: UCI — 2027 WorldTour calendar and safety changes

03 — XCO / XCM

XCO World Cup season wrapped: Rissveds and Martin take the overall crowns

The 2026 WHOOP UCI Mountain Bike World Series concluded at Lake Placid in October. Jenny Rissveds (Sweden) secured the Elite Women’s XCO overall title, while Luca Martin (France) took the Elite Men’s crown.

At the Lake Placid finale, Sina Frei won the women’s XCO race and Charlie Aldridge won the men’s. Frei also secured the Women’s XCC overall title; Martin completed the XCO/XCC overall double in the men’s competition.

MGC take: consistency decided the season. Rissveds and Martin did not need to win the final XCO round to secure the series — they arrived with enough points in hand.

Source: UCI — Lake Placid completes the 2026 MTB World Series

The three-stage FNB Wines2Whales Shiraz concluded on 15 November in South Africa. Jaedon Terlouw and Tristan Nortje (Toyota Imbuko Specialized) won the Elite Men’s general classification in 8:13:17.9.

04 — TECHNOLOGY

Cannondale brings Classified Powershift to the SuperX gravel racer

18 November: Cannondale and Classified announced a partnership, beginning with the SuperX LTD Classified. Its rear hub houses Classified’s two-speed Powershift system, pairing with Shimano GRX Di2 to offer the range of a 2x drivetrain while retaining a single front chainring.

The listed build includes a 46-tooth chainring, an 11–34 12-speed cassette, Reserve carbon wheels, Vittoria Terreno T50 40mm tyres and a 4iiii power meter. Cannondale says the hub shift is controlled through the existing Di2 shifters. The launch announcement lists a €6,999 recommended price; Australian pricing was not confirmed in the material checked for this edition.

Why it matters: this is a significant mainstream-brand adoption step for an alternative to the conventional front derailleur. The real-world questions are weight, serviceability, durability and whether the system’s extra ratio control justifies its cost for gravel racers.

Sources: Cannondale — SuperX LTD Classified specifications · Bicycle Retailer — partnership announcement, 18 November

Van Rysel unveils a 705g RCR-R Pro frame

Van Rysel has officially shown its next-generation RCR-R Pro road frame, with a claimed 705g frame weight and a claimed complete-bike weight of 6.6kg in a SRAM Red build. The brand says aerodynamic performance is comparable to the outgoing model; its separate aero bottle and cage are claimed to save up to 6.3 watts at 45km/h.

The bike is scheduled for market launch on 5 January 2027, with European pricing reported between €3,299 and €8,999 depending on model and build. The weight and aerodynamic figures are manufacturer claims, not independent MGC test results; market-specific Australian pricing remains to be confirmed.

Source: Cycling Weekly — Van Rysel RCR-R Pro unveiled, 12 November
